THE SHORT VERSION01—05

SO, WHAT DO THEY DO?

They turn horsepower
into corner speed.

Tyres are the car’s only contact with the track. Their compound and temperature decide how much braking, cornering and acceleration force the car can put down—and for how long.

That makes a tyre choice a trade: more grip now usually means less performance later. Teams manage that trade every lap.

THE WEEKEND ALLOCATION03—05

HOW MANY DO THEY GET?

A lot of rubber.
Not unlimited.

The FIA and Pirelli set a controlled allocation for each driver. Teams have to choose, save and return sets as the weekend unfolds.

13SETS

Dry tyres per driver

On a standard 2026 Grand Prix weekend, each driver has 13 sets of slicks: typically two Hard, three Medium and eight Soft. One set means four tyres—so that’s up to 52 dry tyres allocated to one driver across the weekend.

DEGRADATION, WITHOUT THE JARGON05—05

WHY TYRES SLOW DOWN

Grip is a budget.
Every lap spends it.

“Degradation” means performance fading over a stint. It can be gradual—or arrive suddenly when a tyre is pushed outside its happy range.

ILLUSTRATIVE PACE THROUGH A STINTFASTER ↑

The pit stop resets the tyre—not the race clock. A faster tyre may be worth the time lost in the pit lane.

∿

The tyre heats, flexes and scrubs against the asphalt on every lap. Teams aim for its working window: too cool and it struggles to grip; too hot and it can overheat, wear or break up.

Drivers manage that temperature with braking, steering, throttle and sometimes by easing off for a few corners. This is why a lap can be quick without being the fastest possible lap.

01Rubber used up

Wear

Every lap abrades and shears away rubber. As the tread thins, the tyre has less rubber to work with and grip falls away.

02Too hot for too long

Thermal fade

Repeated braking, cornering and acceleration put energy into the tyre. Outside its useful temperature window, the compound loses performance faster.

03Surface slips and tears

Graining

When the carcass is cool but the tread surface is hotter, the surface can slide, wrinkle and shed little rolls of rubber. The roughened surface costs grip; it may improve if the tyre settles back into its window.

04Heat from within

Blistering

Excess heat inside the tyre can form bubbles beneath the surface. When they break away, chunks of rubber go with them and the damage is difficult to recover from.

F1 VS. ROAD TYRESEXTRA LAP

BUILT FOR DIFFERENT LIVES

A race tyre isn’t
a road tyre in red.

One is tuned for a narrow, high-performance job on a circuit. The other needs to be dependable through seasons, storms and school runs.

01 / F1 SLICK

Peak grip.
Narrow purpose.

  • Smooth tread for maximum dry contact
  • Designed to work in a carefully managed temperature range
  • Optimised for race pace, not thousands of road kilometres
  • Intermediate and full-wet designs for rain
02 / ROAD TYRE

All-weather.
Long-distance.

  • Grooves channel water and help resist aquaplaning
  • Compound works across a much wider range of conditions
  • Designed for wear life, comfort, noise and everyday safety
  • Built for public roads and changing surfaces

THE TAKEAWAY F1 slicks are extraordinary specialist tools—not a practical or road-legal upgrade for an ordinary car.
